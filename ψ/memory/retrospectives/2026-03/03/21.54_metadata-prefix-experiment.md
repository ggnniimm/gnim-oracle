## AI Diary

This session humbled me. I came in with a clean, logical plan — add more metadata to chunk text, let the embedding model see richer context, watch retrieval improve. On paper it made perfect sense. In practice, it was a disaster.

The first attempt dropped us from 44/44 to 36/44. Eight test cases broke. I thought maybe I was too aggressive, so I stripped it back to just subtopic. Still 38/44. The Gemini embedding model is far more sensitive to prefix text than I expected. Those bracket-enclosed metadata strings aren't just metadata to the model — they dominate the embedding space. A 100-character prefix on a 400-character chunk means 25% of the semantic signal is metadata noise.

The hardest part was accepting that the original "dumb" prefix (`[ref_number | date | category]`) was actually optimal. Sometimes less is more. The category field is genuinely distinctive ("ข้อหารือ กวจ." vs "หนังสือเวียน"), while topic is "การจัดซื้อจัดจ้าง" for 95% of documents — pure noise that dilutes the actual content signal.

The TC-044 fix at the end felt like a consolation prize, but it's actually the right kind of work. Must_contain keywords should be resilient to LLM paraphrasing. "ขยายเวลา" captures the same concept as "ขยายระยะเวลา" but won't break when the model chooses the shorter form.

What I learned: don't enrich embeddings with metadata that has low cardinality. And don't assume more information = better retrieval.

## Honest Feedback

**Friction 1: No rollback safety net.** When I modified `_metadata_prefix()`, every change required a full index rebuild (delete FAISS + dedup DB, re-index 22K chunks, re-index law docs). Each rebuild takes several minutes. There's no way to A/B test a prefix change without committing to it. A "shadow index" or versioned index approach would have saved significant time — I could have compared retrieval results side-by-side without destroying the working index.

**Friction 2: Rate limiting killed confidence.** After reverting the prefix, the first eval run showed 40/44 — but 2 of those failures were rate limit 429 errors, not actual retrieval problems. It took a second run to confirm 42/44, and then targeted TC-033/TC-044 runs to reach confidence. When your eval harness can't distinguish "API quota exhausted" from "retrieval broken," you waste time chasing phantom regressions.

**Friction 3: must_contain brittleness.** TC-044 used `ไม่อาจ` and `ขยายระยะเวลา` as must_contain keywords — both are valid legal Thai but the LLM freely substitutes synonyms ("ไม่สามารถ", "ขยายเวลา"). This is a recurring pattern across the eval suite. A fuzzy matching or semantic similarity check for must_contain would be more robust than exact string matching.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Embedding prefix sensitivity**: Gemini embedding model treats bracket-prefix text as high-weight context. Adding low-cardinality metadata (topic shared by 95% of docs) dilutes the embedding signal and hurts retrieval.
2. **Less is more for chunk enrichment**: The original `[ref_number | date | category]` prefix works because each field has high cardinality and genuine discriminative power.
3. **must_contain should use common LLM phrasing**: Use the shortest, most natural form of a keyword that the LLM is likely to generate. "ขยายเวลา" > "ขยายระยะเวลา".

## Next Steps
