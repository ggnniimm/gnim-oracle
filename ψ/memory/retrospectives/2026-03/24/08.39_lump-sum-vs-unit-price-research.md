## AI Diary

This was a deeply satisfying research session despite ending without finding "the document." Two full sessions — probably 150+ minutes total — searching through hundreds of pages of Thai government documents, Google Drive folders, web searches, FIDIC documentation, and academic materials. The answer turned out to be what I suspected midway through the first session: there is no single Thai government document that says "เหมารวมใช้เมื่อแบบรูปรายการสมบูรณ์ ปริมาณงานแน่นอน."

What I find interesting is how this knowledge propagates. FIDIC establishes that Red Book (re-measurement) is for employer-designed projects and Yellow Book (lump sum) is for contractor-designed projects. Thai construction practitioners and academics then synthesize this into a simpler heuristic: "lump sum when drawings are complete and quantities are certain." Industry websites like at-once.info and mangoconsultant.com repeat this as settled knowledge — but none cite a source, because there isn't one document to cite. It's emergent professional knowledge.

The humbling part is how confidently AI systems (including me, potentially) could state this as if it came from a specific regulation. Ming was right to be suspicious — "AI บอกมา" — and right to demand the documentary source. The answer "it's FIDIC-derived industry practice, not a Thai regulation" is honest and useful. It means Ming can use this knowledge in RAG answers but should frame it as "หลักปฏิบัติทั่วไปในงานก่อสร้าง" rather than citing a specific circular.

## Honest Feedback

**1. Should have concluded faster.** By the second search of the 282-page กรมบัญชีกลาง document returning zero results, the pattern was clear: Thai government documents define HOW to calculate prices and provide contract TEMPLATES, but don't prescribe WHEN to use which type. I could have pivoted to FIDIC 20 minutes earlier and saved Ming time.

**2. Web search was less efficient than it could be.** I made 6+ web searches with overlapping queries. A more strategic approach would have been: (1) search Thai government sites first, (2) if nothing, search for the concept in English (FIDIC/international), (3) confirm. Instead I kept trying variations of Thai queries hoping to find a government document that doesn't exist.

**3. The FIDIC evidence could be stronger.** When Ming asked "ขอดูหลักฐานของ FIDIC," I showed the contract selection guide — but FIDIC itself doesn't explicitly say "lump sum when drawings complete." It says Red Book = employer design with re-measurement, Yellow Book = contractor design with lump sum. The inference is logical but I should have been more explicit that even FIDIC doesn't state the criteria in exactly those words. The statement is a practitioner's synthesis of FIDIC principles, not a FIDIC quote.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Thai government procurement docs prescribe HOW, not WHEN**: พ.ร.บ./ระเบียบ/หลักเกณฑ์ define procedures, templates, and calculation methods. Contract type selection is left to the discretion of the procuring agency. No circular mandates when to use lump sum vs unit price.
2. **AI-generated "common knowledge" often has no single source**: When an AI states criteria that sound authoritative, the source may be synthesized from multiple principles (like FIDIC) rather than a single document. Always ask for the specific citation.
